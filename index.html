Why Space Experiments Demand
Autonomous On-board AI.

As humanity ventures to the BAS (Bharatiya Antariksh Station), the Moon, and beyond, real-time ground control assistance becomes impossible due to transmission light-delays and restricted communications bandwidth. The on-board HAR system observes astronaut actions through fixed-payload cameras, validates protocols locally, and provides instantaneous voice feedback.

04 / ADVANCED MODULE (OPTIONAL)

Orientation-Agnostic
3D Human Mesh Recovery.

Standard 2D and floor-based 3D posture models fail in microgravity because astronauts operate without a fixed "up" or "down" gravity vector. This optional advanced challenge implements an orientation-agnostic 3D Human Mesh Recovery (HMR) pipeline where body pose is tracked relative to the payload rack rather than a terrestrial floor.

ADVANCED / OPTIONAL CHALLENGE MODULE
PAYLOAD RACK REFERENCE FRAME (RRF)
● RACK REFERENCE FRAME: FIXED XYZ
FRAME: PAYLOAD_RACK_COORD
BODY POSE: RELATIVE_TO_RACK
ROLL: 0.0° | PITCH: 0.0°
PRINCIPLE: BODY ORIENTATION ≠ FLOOR ORIENTATION

Instead of projecting keypoints onto a virtual ground plane, the AI extracts a full 3D body mesh and computes joint angles with respect to coordinate axes [X_rack, Y_rack, Z_rack] anchored directly to the physical payload enclosure.

05 / STANDALONE EDGE ARCHITECTURE

Process Locally.
Communicate Minimally.

Space station and lunar habitats operate under severe bandwidth constraints. High-definition raw video is never streamed back to Earth for inference; instead, local edge accelerators analyze every frame in-situ, producing lightweight structured text logs and local archives.
